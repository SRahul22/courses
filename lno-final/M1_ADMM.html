M1 · ADMM (Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers)

Chapter 10 (notes §10.6–10.7) · about 60–75 min · Goal: given any $f_1$, $f_2$ and a coupling constraint, write the augmented Lagrangian and derive the exact formula for all three ADMM steps. He promised a "hard problem" on exactly this.

1. Must remember

Memorise cold
  • Problem: $\min_{x_1,x_2}\ f_1(x_1)+f_2(x_2)$ s.t. $A(x_1,x_2)=A_1x_1+A_2x_2-b=0$.
  • Augmented Lagrangian ($\beta>0$ fixed): $$L_A(x_1,x_2,\lambda)=f_1(x_1)+f_2(x_2)+\lambda^T A(x_1,x_2)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|A(x_1,x_2)\|_2^2$$
  • One ADMM iteration (order matters): $$\begin{aligned} x_1^{(k+1)}&\in\arg\min_{x_1} L_A(x_1,\,x_2^{(k)},\,\lambda^{(k)})\\ x_2^{(k+1)}&\in\arg\min_{x_2} L_A(x_1^{(k+1)},\,x_2,\,\lambda^{(k)})\\ \lambda^{(k+1)}&=\lambda^{(k)}+\beta\,A(x_1^{(k+1)},x_2^{(k+1)}) \end{aligned}$$ The $x_2$-step uses the new $x_1$ and the old $\lambda$.
  • Completing the square (the one identity you use every time): $$\lambda^T(z-c)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-c\|^2=\frac{\beta}{2}\Big\|z-\big(c-\tfrac{\lambda}{\beta}\big)\Big\|^2-\frac{\|\lambda\|^2}{2\beta}$$ If the sign inside is flipped ($\lambda^T(c-z)$), the centre is $c+\lambda/\beta$.
  • Soft thresholding: $\arg\min_z\ \gamma|z|+\frac{\beta}{2}(z-y)^2=S_{\gamma/\beta}(y)$, with $S_\tau(t)=\operatorname{sign}(t)\max(|t|-\tau,0)$, i.e. $t-\tau$ if $t>\tau$; $0$ if $|t|\le\tau$; $t+\tau$ if $t<-\tau$.
  • Indicator → projection: $\arg\min_v\ P_C(v)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|v-y\|^2=\Pi_C(y)$. Box $[0,B]^n$: clip each component, $\min(\max(y_i,0),B)$. Orthant: $\max(y_i,0)$.
  • LASSO answer ($\min \frac12\|Mx-b\|^2+\gamma\|x\|_1$, split $x_1-x_2=0$): $$\begin{aligned} (M^TM+\beta I)\,x_1^{(k+1)}&=M^Tb+\beta x_2^{(k)}-\lambda^{(k)}\\ x_2^{(k+1)}&=S_{\gamma/\beta}\big(x_1^{(k+1)}+\lambda^{(k)}/\beta\big)\\ \lambda^{(k+1)}&=\lambda^{(k)}+\beta\,(x_1^{(k+1)}-x_2^{(k+1)}) \end{aligned}$$

2. Concepts

2.1 The problem ADMM solves

In plain words: you have a cost made of two pieces that are each easy on their own but hard together (e.g. a smooth least-squares piece plus a non-smooth $|\cdot|$ piece, or a piece plus a constraint). ADMM gives each piece its own copy of the variable, forces the copies to agree with an equality constraint, and then handles one piece at a time.

Precisely (notes §10.6.1): $\min f_1(x_1)+f_2(x_2)$ s.t. $A_1x_1+A_2x_2-b=0$, with $x_i\in\mathbb{R}^{n_i}$, $A_i\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n_i}$, $b\in\mathbb{R}^r$. Each $f_i:\mathbb{R}^{n_i}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$ is proper, closed, convex, possibly non-smooth. The value $+\infty$ is how a constraint is written into $f_i$ (indicator function). The objective separates; only the affine constraint couples the blocks.

Splitting = choosing $A_1, A_2, b$
  • LASSO, copies agree: $x_1-x_2=0$ → $A_1=I,\ A_2=-I,\ b=0$.
  • Penalty on $Dx$ (e.g. differences): $Dx_1-x_2=0$ → $A_1=D,\ A_2=-I,\ b=0$.
  • Two copies for two extra pieces: $\begin{bmatrix}x_1\\x_1\end{bmatrix}-x_2=0$ → $A_1=\begin{bmatrix}I\\I\end{bmatrix},\ A_2=-I_{2n},\ b=0$ (still two blocks: $x_2=(u,v)$ is one block).

2.2 Lagrangian, dual, and why "augmented"

In plain words: the plain Lagrangian $L=f_1+f_2+\lambda^TA(x_1,x_2)$ gives a dual problem, but "dual ascent" on it can converge slowly and its $x$-minimiser may not even be feasible. Adding the penalty $\frac{\beta}{2}\|A(x_1,x_2)\|^2$ punishes constraint violation, and the multiplier term carries dual information from one iteration to the next.

Precisely: $q(\lambda)=\inf_{x_1,x_2}L(x_1,x_2,\lambda)$, dual problem $\max_\lambda q(\lambda)$. $L_A=L+\frac{\beta}{2}\|A(x_1,x_2)\|_2^2$ with fixed $\beta>0$.

2.3 The 3-step iteration

In plain words: minimise $L_A$ over $x_1$ (freeze the rest), then over $x_2$ using the fresh $x_1$, then nudge $\lambda$ in the direction of the constraint residual, with step $\beta$.

Notation used in the notes: $f_{1,k}(z)=L_A(z,x_2^{(k)},\lambda^{(k)})$ and $f_{2,k}(z)=L_A(x_1^{(k+1)},z,\lambda^{(k)})$. Drop every term that doesn't involve the variable you are minimising over (write "+ const").

Residual $r^{(k+1)}=A(x_1^{(k+1)},x_2^{(k+1)})$; update $\lambda^{(k+1)}=\lambda^{(k)}+\beta r^{(k+1)}$. Initialise each block where its $f_i$ is finite; any $\lambda^{(0)}$. If a subproblem has several minimisers, pick any.

Trap In the $x_2$-step, using $x_1^{(k)}$ (old) or $\lambda^{(k+1)}$ (new) is wrong. It's new $x_1$, old $\lambda$. The multiplier step size is $\beta$ itself, the same $\beta$ as in the penalty.

2.4 Completing the square (the core skill)

In plain words: a linear term plus a squared distance is just a squared distance to a shifted centre. Once you see that, every ADMM subproblem becomes "$f_i$ + squared distance to a point", which is easy.

Derivation (verify by expanding — he said "these are the exercises you should do")

$$\frac{\beta}{2}\Big\|z-c+\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\Big\|^2=\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-c\|^2+\lambda^T(z-c)+\frac{\|\lambda\|^2}{2\beta}.$$ So $\lambda^T(z-c)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-c\|^2=\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-a\|^2-\frac{\|\lambda\|^2}{2\beta}$ with centre $a=c-\lambda/\beta$. The last term doesn't depend on $z$ → it's a constant.

  • $x_1$-step of LASSO: $\lambda^T(z-x_2^{(k)})+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-x_2^{(k)}\|^2$ → centre $a_k=x_2^{(k)}-\lambda^{(k)}/\beta$.
  • $x_2$-step of LASSO: $\lambda^T(x_1^{(k+1)}-z)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-x_1^{(k+1)}\|^2$ (note the minus sign on $z$) → centre $y=x_1^{(k+1)}+\lambda^{(k)}/\beta$.
  • General $A$: $\lambda^T(Dz-w)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|Dz-w\|^2=\frac{\beta}{2}\|Dz-w+\lambda/\beta\|^2+$ const.

2.5 Soft thresholding (and his graphical proof)

In plain words: minimising $\gamma|z|+\frac{\beta}{2}(z-y)^2$ pulls $y$ towards 0 by the amount $\tau=\gamma/\beta$, and if $y$ is within $\tau$ of zero it snaps exactly to 0. That's why LASSO gives sparse answers.

Proof as done in class (no subgradients, graph + Ch 6/1-D calculus)
  1. Separate: $\gamma\|z\|_1+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-y\|^2=\sum_i\big[\gamma|z_i|+\frac{\beta}{2}(z_i-y_i)^2\big]$, so solve one scalar problem $p(z)=\gamma|z|+\frac{\beta}{2}(z-y)^2$. Divide by $\beta$: same minimiser as $\tau|z|+\frac12(z-y)^2$, $\tau=\gamma/\beta$.
  2. Case $y>0$. For $z<0$ both terms increase as $z$ decreases (the function is monotonically increasing to the left of 0); for $z>y$ both increase as $z$ increases. So the minimiser $z^*\in[0,y]$, in particular $z^*\ge0$.
  3. So $z^*=\bar z:=\arg\min_{z\ge0}\ \tau z+\frac12(z-y)^2$ (on $z\ge0$, $|z|=z$, so it's now differentiable with a constraint).
  4. Drop the constraint: $\hat z$ solves $\tau+(z-y)=0$, so $\hat z=y-\tau$. If $\hat z\ge0$ (i.e. $y\ge\tau$), it's feasible, so $\bar z=\hat z=y-\tau$.
  5. If $\hat z<0$ (i.e. $0<y<\tau$): the convex function $\tau z+\frac12(z-y)^2$ is increasing on $[0,\infty)$ (its minimum is to the left of 0), so $\bar z=0$.
  6. Case $y<0$: mirror image; $z^*=y+\tau$ if $y\le-\tau$, else 0. ($y=0$ gives $z^*=0$.)
  7. Combine: $z^*=\operatorname{sign}(y)\max(|y|-\tau,0)=S_\tau(y)$.

Graph of $S_\tau$: flat at 0 on $[-\tau,\tau]$, then slope-1 lines $y-\tau$ (right) and $y+\tau$ (left).

Trap The threshold is $\gamma/\beta$, not $\gamma$ and not $\beta/\gamma$. If the quadratic has a different coefficient, e.g. $\gamma|z|+\frac{c}{2}(z-y)^2$, the threshold is $\gamma/c$.

2.6 Example 1: LASSO (notes §10.7.1), derived fully

$\min_x \frac12\|Mx-b\|_2^2+\gamma\|x\|_1$, $M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$, $\gamma>0$.

Full derivation — this is the answer template
  1. Split: $f_1(x_1)=\frac12\|Mx_1-b\|^2$, $f_2(x_2)=\gamma\|x_2\|_1$, constraint $x_1-x_2=0$ (so $A_1=I$, $A_2=-I$, $b=0$). Equivalent to LASSO because $x_1=x_2$.
  2. Augmented Lagrangian: $L_A=\frac12\|Mx_1-b\|^2+\gamma\|x_2\|_1+\lambda^T(x_1-x_2)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|x_1-x_2\|^2$.
  3. $x_1$-step: $f_{1,k}(z)=\frac12\|Mz-b\|^2+\lambda^{(k)T}(z-x_2^{(k)})+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-x_2^{(k)}\|^2=\frac12\|Mz-b\|^2+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-a_k\|^2+$ const, with $a_k=x_2^{(k)}-\lambda^{(k)}/\beta$.
    Smooth and strongly convex → set the gradient to zero: $M^T(Mz-b)+\beta(z-a_k)=0$ ⇒ $(M^TM+\beta I)z=M^Tb+\beta a_k=M^Tb+\beta x_2^{(k)}-\lambda^{(k)}$. The matrix $M^TM+\beta I$ is PD since $\beta>0$, so the solution is unique: $x_1^{(k+1)}=z^\star$.
  4. $x_2$-step: $f_{2,k}(z)=\gamma\|z\|_1+\lambda^{(k)T}(x_1^{(k+1)}-z)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-x_1^{(k+1)}\|^2=\gamma\|z\|_1+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-y\|^2+$ const, with $y=x_1^{(k+1)}+\lambda^{(k)}/\beta$.
    Separates by component → $x_{2,i}^{(k+1)}=S_{\gamma/\beta}(y_i)$.
  5. Multiplier: $\lambda^{(k+1)}=\lambda^{(k)}+\beta(x_1^{(k+1)}-x_2^{(k+1)})$.

So one primal step is a linear solve, the other is soft thresholding.

2.7 Example 2: adding a constraint via an indicator (notes §10.7.2)

$\min_x \|Mx-b\|_2^2+\gamma\|x\|_1+P(x)$, where $P(x)=0$ if $x\in C$, $+\infty$ otherwise ($C$ nonempty, closed, convex; e.g. box or orthant). Note: no ½ on the data term here.

Full derivation
  1. Split with two copies: $x_1\in\mathbb{R}^n$, $x_2=\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}$. $f_1(x_1)=\|Mx_1-b\|^2$, $f_2(x_2)=\gamma\|u\|_1+P(v)$, constraint $\begin{bmatrix}x_1\\x_1\end{bmatrix}-x_2=0$ (i.e. $x_1=u$, $x_1=v$). Still a two-block ADMM: $f_2$ is a single function of the single block $x_2$, and the two pieces separate inside it.
  2. Multiplier blocks: $\lambda=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_u\\\lambda_v\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}$. Residual $r=\begin{bmatrix}x_1-u\\x_1-v\end{bmatrix}$. $L_A=\|Mx_1-b\|^2+\gamma\|u\|_1+P(v)+\lambda^Tr+\frac{\beta}{2}\|r\|^2$.
  3. $x_1$-step: complete the square in each residual block: centres $a_k=u^{(k)}-\lambda_u^{(k)}/\beta$, $b_k=v^{(k)}-\lambda_v^{(k)}/\beta$: $f_{1,k}(z)=\|Mz-b\|^2+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-a_k\|^2+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-b_k\|^2+$ const.
    Gradient: $2M^T(Mz-b)+\beta(z-a_k)+\beta(z-b_k)=0$ (the 2 comes from the data term having no ½) ⇒ $$(M^TM+\beta I)z^\star=M^Tb+\frac{\beta}{2}(a_k+b_k).$$
  4. $x_2$-step: centres $y_u=x_1^{(k+1)}+\lambda_u^{(k)}/\beta$, $y_v=x_1^{(k+1)}+\lambda_v^{(k)}/\beta$. Objective separates: $\big[\gamma\|u\|_1+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u-y_u\|^2\big]+\big[P(v)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|v-y_v\|^2\big]$. Two independent problems:
    • $u^{(k+1)}=S_{\gamma/\beta}(y_u)$ componentwise.
    • $v^{(k+1)}=\arg\min_{v\in C}\|v-y_v\|^2=\Pi_C(y_v)$ (unique since $C$ is closed convex). Orthant: $\max(y_{v,i},0)$. Box $[0,B]^n$: clip to $[0,B]$.
  5. Multipliers: $\lambda_u^{(k+1)}=\lambda_u^{(k)}+\beta(x_1^{(k+1)}-u^{(k+1)})$, $\lambda_v^{(k+1)}=\lambda_v^{(k)}+\beta(x_1^{(k+1)}-v^{(k+1)})$.
Traps in Example 2
  • Writing $(M^TM+\beta I)z=M^Tb+\beta(a_k+b_k)$ (forgot to divide the $\beta$ by 2), or $(M^TM+2\beta I)$ with a $\frac12$ data term. Redo the gradient each time; don't copy LASSO's formula.
  • Box projection = clipping. Why (he said "you can prove it using the first order optimization of chapter six"): for $\min (v_i-y_i)^2$ s.t. $0\le v_i\le B$, the KKT/tangent-cone condition gives $v_i=y_i$ if inside, else the nearer bound.
  • He said he will NOT give an arbitrary $C$: expect a box, an orthant, or something equally simple.

2.8 Theory around ADMM (likely MCQ / short answer)

  • Primal recovery under strong duality (links to Ch 7): if primal and dual optima are attained and $p^*=d^*$, every primal optimum minimises $L(\cdot,\lambda^*)$. But a minimiser of $L(\cdot,\lambda^*)$ need not be feasible. A feasible minimiser is primal optimal. If the minimiser is unique, it equals the primal optimum (so it's feasible). Strict convexity of $f_1+f_2$ suffices for uniqueness (the $\lambda$-term is affine).
  • Why augment: plain dual ascent can be slow; the penalty $\frac{\beta}{2}\|A\|^2$ penalises violation and makes the subproblems strongly convex in the coupled part.
  • Why alternate (not joint): each block minimisation is often easy (linear solve, closed-form prox) while the joint one is not.
  • Convergence (assumptions: proper closed convex $f_i$, the ordinary Lagrangian has a saddle point, subproblems attain their minimum, fixed $\beta$, exact solves; conclusions: residual → 0, objective → $p^*$, multipliers → a dual optimum). He said he will not ask this; know it exists, nothing more.

2.9 The exam recipe (use this on ANY new $f_1$, $f_2$)

Recipe
  1. Write the split: name $f_1$, $f_2$, the constraint $A_1x_1+A_2x_2=b$. Say why it's equivalent to the original.
  2. Write $L_A$ in full.
  3. $x_1$-step: write $f_{1,k}(z)$, drop constants, complete the square (state the centre), then either set the gradient to 0 (smooth $f_1$ → linear system; say why the matrix is PD) or recognise a prox (soft-threshold / projection).
  4. $x_2$-step: same, with new $x_1$, old $\lambda$. If it separates by component or by sub-block, say so explicitly.
  5. $\lambda$-step: $\lambda+\beta\times$residual. Write the residual for your $A_1, A_2, b$.
  6. Box the final three formulas.

Q1 NewMCQ · warm-up

For fixed $\lambda$, $\beta>0$ and $w$, the function $\lambda^T(z-w)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-w\|^2$ equals $\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-a\|^2+$ const with

  1. (a) $a=w+\lambda/\beta$
  2. (b) $a=w-\beta\lambda$
  3. (c) $a=w-\lambda/\beta$
  4. (d) $a=w-\lambda$
Show answer

(c). Expand $\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-w+\lambda/\beta\|^2=\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-w\|^2+\lambda^T(z-w)+\frac{\|\lambda\|^2}{2\beta}$. So the centre is $w-\lambda/\beta$. (a) is the centre when the linear term is $\lambda^T(w-z)$ — the $x_2$-step situation. (b), (d) have the wrong scaling: the shift must be $\lambda/\beta$ so that $\beta\cdot$shift $=\lambda$.

Q2 NewMCQ · warm-up

With $\gamma=1$, $\beta=2$: $S_{\gamma/\beta}$ applied componentwise to $y=(1.2,\,-0.3,\,-2,\,0.5)$ gives

  1. (a) $(0.2,\,0,\,-1,\,0)$
  2. (b) $(0.7,\,0,\,-1.5,\,0)$
  3. (c) $(0.7,\,-0.3,\,-1.5,\,0)$
  4. (d) $(0.7,\,0.2,\,-2.5,\,0)$
Show answer

(b). Threshold $\tau=\gamma/\beta=0.5$. $1.2\to0.7$; $|-0.3|\le0.5\to0$; $-2\to-1.5$; $|0.5|\le0.5\to0$. (a) used $\tau=\gamma=1$. (c) forgot to zero out the small component. (d) pushed away from zero — that's the wrong direction (and changes sign for $-0.3$).

Q3 NewMCQ

In one ADMM iteration, the $x_2$-update minimises

  1. (a) $L_A(x_1^{(k+1)},x_2,\lambda^{(k)})$
  2. (b) $L_A(x_1^{(k)},x_2,\lambda^{(k)})$
  3. (c) $L_A(x_1^{(k+1)},x_2,\lambda^{(k+1)})$
  4. (d) $L(x_1^{(k+1)},x_2,\lambda^{(k)})$ (the ordinary Lagrangian)
Show answer

(a). Gauss–Seidel order: use the freshly computed $x_1^{(k+1)}$; $\lambda$ is updated only after both primal steps. (b) would be a Jacobi (parallel) update — not ADMM. (c): $\lambda^{(k+1)}$ doesn't exist yet. (d) drops the penalty; then the step may be unbounded or not unique.

Q4 NewMCQ

Let $C=[0,2]^3$ and $P$ its indicator. Then $\arg\min_v\ P(v)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|v-y\|^2$ for $y=(-1,\,0.4,\,3)$ is

  1. (a) $(0,\,0.4,\,3)$
  2. (b) $(-1,\,0.4,\,2)$
  3. (c) depends on $\beta$
  4. (d) $(0,\,0.4,\,2)$
Show answer

(d). $P$ restricts $v$ to $C$, so the problem is $\min_{v\in C}\|v-y\|^2$ = projection onto the box = clip each component to $[0,2]$. $\beta>0$ only scales the objective, so it doesn't change the minimiser (not (c)). (a), (b) clip only one side.

Q6 New, theory (links Ch 7)MCQ

Assume strong duality with both optima attained, and let $\lambda^*$ be dual optimal. Which statement is correct in general?

  1. (a) Every minimiser of $L(\cdot,\lambda^*)$ is primal optimal.
  2. (b) If $L(\cdot,\lambda^*)$ has a unique minimiser, it is primal optimal.
  3. (c) Primal recovery needs both $f_1$ and $f_2$ to be differentiable.
  4. (d) A minimiser of $L(\cdot,\lambda^*)$ is always feasible.
Show answer

(b). Every primal optimum minimises $L(\cdot,\lambda^*)$; if that minimiser is unique, the primal optimum must be it. (a)/(d) are false: e.g. $\min x$ s.t. $-x\le0$ (Ch 7) — $L(\cdot,\mu^*)$ is constant, every $x$ minimises it, including infeasible ones. (c) is irrelevant: the result uses only optimality and attainment.

Q7 New — "derive the steps" (the promised type)Descriptive · exam level

Consider the box-constrained least-squares problem $\min_x \frac12\|Mx-b\|_2^2$ s.t. $x\in[0,B]^n$. (a) Write it as a two-block ADMM problem (state $f_1$, $f_2$, $A_1$, $A_2$, $b$). (b) Write $L_A$. (c) Derive closed-form $x_1$-, $x_2$-, $\lambda$-updates.

Hint

Put the constraint into $f_2$ as an indicator. The $x_2$-step is a projection.

Show answer
  1. (a) $f_1(x_1)=\frac12\|Mx_1-b\|^2$, $f_2(x_2)=P(x_2)$ = indicator of $C=[0,B]^n$. Constraint $x_1-x_2=0$: $A_1=I$, $A_2=-I$, $b=0$. Equivalent since $x_1=x_2\in C$.
  2. (b) $L_A=\frac12\|Mx_1-b\|^2+P(x_2)+\lambda^T(x_1-x_2)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|x_1-x_2\|^2$.
  3. (c) $x_1$: $f_{1,k}(z)=\frac12\|Mz-b\|^2+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-a_k\|^2+$const, $a_k=x_2^{(k)}-\lambda^{(k)}/\beta$. Gradient $=0$: $\boxed{(M^TM+\beta I)x_1^{(k+1)}=M^Tb+\beta x_2^{(k)}-\lambda^{(k)}}$ (PD matrix ⇒ unique).
  4. $x_2$: $f_{2,k}(z)=P(z)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-y\|^2+$const, $y=x_1^{(k+1)}+\lambda^{(k)}/\beta$ ⇒ $\boxed{x_{2,i}^{(k+1)}=\min\big(\max(y_i,0),B\big)}$ (projection onto the box = clipping).
  5. $\boxed{\lambda^{(k+1)}=\lambda^{(k)}+\beta(x_1^{(k+1)}-x_2^{(k+1)})}$.

Checked numerically: the iteration converges to the same answer as a bounded least-squares solver.

Q13 New — Example 2 with a box, fullyDescriptive · exam level

Derive the complete ADMM iteration for $\min_x \|Mx-b\|^2+\gamma\|x\|_1$ s.t. $0\le x_i\le B$ using the two-copy split $x_2=(u,v)$. Explain why it is two-block even though $x_2\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}$.

Show answer
  1. $f_1(x_1)=\|Mx_1-b\|^2$, $f_2(u,v)=\gamma\|u\|_1+P_{[0,B]^n}(v)$, constraint $x_1-u=0$, $x_1-v=0$. Multipliers $\lambda_u,\lambda_v$.
