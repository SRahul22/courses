Part A · Short answers (compulsory, no sheet, 10 × 2 = 20 marks)

A1 LP duality2 marks

Write the dual of the standard-form LP $\;\min_x c^Tx$ subject to $Ax=b,\ x\ge 0$, stating the sign of the dual variable.

Show answer

$$\max_{\nu}\ b^T\nu\quad\text{subject to}\quad A^T\nu\le c,\qquad \nu\ \text{free (unrestricted)}.$$ (From $L=c^Tx+\nu^T(b-Ax)-\mu^Tx$, $\mu\ge0$. With the course's $L=f+\lambda^T(Ax-b)$ convention, $\lambda=-\nu$, giving $\max -b^T\lambda$ s.t. $-A^T\lambda\le c$: the same problem.)

A2 LP KKT2 marks

Write the complete KKT (optimality) system for the standard-form LP of A1.

Show answer

$$A^T\nu+\mu=c\ \ \text{(stationarity)},\qquad Ax=b,\ x\ge0\ \ \text{(primal feasibility)},\qquad \mu\ge 0\ \ \text{(dual feasibility)},\qquad \mu_ix_i=0\ \forall i\ \ \text{(complementary slackness)}.$$ Equivalently: $x$ primal feasible, $\nu$ dual feasible ($A^T\nu\le c$), and $x^T(c-A^T\nu)=0$. "Stationarity is the only thing you have to memorise" — the rest is feasibility.

A3 Ch 6 KKT2 marks

For $\min f(x)$ s.t. $h(x)=0$, $g(x)\le 0$, with column-gradient Jacobians $J_h, J_g$, write all five parts of the KKT conditions at $(x^*,\lambda^*,\mu^*)$.

Show answer
  1. Stationarity: $\nabla f(x^*)+J_h(x^*)\lambda^*+J_g(x^*)\mu^*=0$.
  2. Equality feasibility: $h(x^*)=0$.
  3. Inequality feasibility: $g(x^*)\le 0$.
  4. Dual feasibility: $\mu^*\ge 0$ ($\lambda^*$ free).
  5. Complementary slackness: $\mu_i^*g_i(x^*)=0$ for every $i$.

Trap: CS is "$=0$", not "$\le 0$"; $\mu\ge0$, not $\le0$ (Midterm 2 I.1 distractors).

A4 Ch 6 second order2 marks

State the second-order sufficient condition (SOSC), including the definition of the critical cone.

Show answer

Let $(x^*,\lambda^*,\mu^*)$ satisfy KKT. Let $A_+=\{j \text{ active}: \mu_j^*>0\}$, $A_0=\{j\text{ active}:\mu_j^*=0\}$ and $$C(x^*)=\{d:\ \nabla h_i(x^*)^Td=0\ \forall i,\ \ \nabla g_j(x^*)^Td=0\ (j\in A_+),\ \ \nabla g_j(x^*)^Td\le 0\ (j\in A_0)\}.$$ If $d^T\nabla^2_{xx}L(x^*,\lambda^*,\mu^*)\,d>0$ for every $d\in C(x^*)$, $d\ne0$, then $x^*$ is a strict local minimiser. (If $C=\{0\}$ the condition holds vacuously. No LICQ needed for SOSC.)

A5 Ch 7 Slater2 marks

$f,g_i$ convex, $h$ affine, a point $x^0$ with $h(x^0)=0$, $g(x^0)<0$, and $p^*$ finite. What does Slater's theorem conclude?

Show answer

Zero duality gap, $p^*=d^*$, and the dual optimum is attained (some $(\lambda^*,\mu^*)$ with $\mu^*\ge0$ has $q(\lambda^*,\mu^*)=d^*$). It does not guarantee that the primal optimum is attained.

A6 Ch 7 weak duality2 marks

State weak duality and say which assumptions it needs.

Show answer

For every primal-feasible $x$ and every $(\lambda,\mu)$ with $\mu\ge0$: $q(\lambda,\mu)\le f(x)$, hence $d^*\le p^*$. Proof in one line: $q(\lambda,\mu)\le L(x,\lambda,\mu)=f(x)+\lambda^Th(x)+\mu^Tg(x)\le f(x)$. No convexity needed.

A7 Ch 10 ADMM2 marks

For $\min f_1(x_1)+f_2(x_2)$ s.t. $A_1x_1+A_2x_2=b$, write the augmented Lagrangian and the three ADMM steps.

Show answer

$L_A=f_1(x_1)+f_2(x_2)+\lambda^T(A_1x_1+A_2x_2-b)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|A_1x_1+A_2x_2-b\|^2$, $\beta>0$. $$x_1^{(k+1)}\in\arg\min_{x_1}L_A(x_1,x_2^{(k)},\lambda^{(k)}),\quad x_2^{(k+1)}\in\arg\min_{x_2}L_A(x_1^{(k+1)},x_2,\lambda^{(k)}),\quad \lambda^{(k+1)}=\lambda^{(k)}+\beta\,(A_1x_1^{(k+1)}+A_2x_2^{(k+1)}-b).$$ New $x_1$, old $\lambda$ in the $x_2$-step; the multiplier step size is the same $\beta$.

A8 Ch 10 soft thresholding2 marks

Give $\arg\min_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\ \gamma|z|+\frac{\beta}{2}(z-v)^2$ ($\gamma,\beta>0$) as an explicit formula.

Show answer

$z^*=S_{\tau}(v)=\operatorname{sign}(v)\max(|v|-\tau,0)$ with $\tau=\gamma/\beta$: $\ v-\tau$ if $v>\tau$; $\ 0$ if $|v|\le\tau$; $\ v+\tau$ if $v<-\tau$.

A9 Ch 4 CG2 marks

For CG on $f=\frac12x^TQx-b^Tx$ ($Q\succ0$), give $\alpha_k$, $\beta_k$ and the direction update, with $g_k=\nabla f(x_k)$.

Show answer

$d_0=-g_0$; $\ \alpha_k=\dfrac{g_k^Tg_k}{d_k^TQd_k}$; $\ x_{k+1}=x_k+\alpha_kd_k$; $\ g_{k+1}=g_k+\alpha_kQd_k$; $\ \beta_k=\dfrac{\|g_{k+1}\|^2}{\|g_k\|^2}$; $\ d_{k+1}=-g_{k+1}+\beta_kd_k$. Gradients mutually orthogonal, directions $Q$-conjugate.

A10 Ch 3 GD on a quadratic2 marks

Fixed-step gradient descent $x_{k+1}=x_k-\alpha\nabla f(x_k)$ on $f=\frac12x^TQx-b^Tx$, $Q\succ 0$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_{\min},\dots,\lambda_{\max}$: for which $\alpha$ does it converge from every start? Best $\alpha$ and its rate?

Show answer

The error in eigen-direction $i$ is multiplied by $(1-\alpha\lambda_i)$ each step, so convergence for every start $\iff |1-\alpha\lambda_i|<1\ \forall i\iff 0<\alpha<2/\lambda_{\max}$. Best fixed step $\alpha^*=2/(\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max})$, rate $\dfrac{\kappa-1}{\kappa+1}$ with $\kappa=\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$.

I.1 Ch 1 trapMCQ

Let $f(x)=x^4$ on $\mathbb{R}$ and $x^*=0$. Which statement is correct?

  1. (a) $x^*$ fails SONC because $f''(0)=0$.
  2. (b) $x^*$ fails SOSC, so it cannot be a strict local minimiser.
  3. (c) $x^*$ satisfies FONC and SONC, fails SOSC, and is nevertheless a strict global minimiser.
  4. (d) $x^*$ satisfies SOSC because $f''(0)\ge 0$.
Show answer

Answer: (c). $f'(0)=0$, $f''(0)=0\ge0$ (FONC, SONC hold); SOSC needs $f''(0)>0$, which fails. Still $x^4>0$ for $x\ne0$, so 0 is a strict global minimiser: SOSC is sufficient, not necessary. (a) SONC only needs $\ge0$. (b) confuses "sufficient" with "necessary". (d) SOSC needs strict positivity.

Let $f(x_1,x_2)=x_1^3+x_2^2$ and $x^*=0$. Which statement is correct?

  1. (a) FONC and SONC hold at $0$, but $0$ is not a local minimiser.
  2. (b) FONC fails at $0$.
  3. (c) FONC and SONC hold, hence $0$ is a local minimiser.
  4. (d) SOSC holds because $\nabla^2f(0)=\operatorname{diag}(0,2)$ is positive semidefinite.

Answer: (a). $\nabla f(0)=(0,0)$, $\nabla^2f(0)=\operatorname{diag}(0,2)\succeq0$, so FONC and SONC hold. But $f(-t,0)=-t^3<0=f(0)$ for every $t>0$, so 0 is not a local minimiser. (b) gradient is zero. (c) necessary conditions are not sufficient. (d) SOSC needs positive definite.

I.4 Ch 3 GD on a quadraticMCQ

Gradient descent with fixed step $\alpha$ on $f(x)=\frac12x^TQx$, $Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,4,10)$. Which statement is correct for every starting point?

  1. (a) It converges iff $0<\alpha<2$; the best fixed step is $\alpha=1/10$.
  2. (b) It converges iff $0<\alpha<0.2$; the best fixed step is $\alpha=2/11$, with worst-case contraction factor $9/11$.
  3. (c) It converges iff $0<\alpha\le0.2$; the best fixed step is $\alpha=2/11$, with factor $10/11$.
  4. (d) It converges iff $0<\alpha<0.2$; the best fixed step is $\alpha=1/10$, with factor $9/10$.
Show answer

Answer: (b). Mode $i$ is multiplied by $1-\alpha\lambda_i$; need $|1-10\alpha|<1\iff\alpha<0.2$. Best $\alpha=2/(1+10)=2/11$ balances $|1-\alpha\cdot1|=|1-10\alpha|=9/11=(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$ with $\kappa=10$. (a) uses $\lambda_{\min}$ instead of $\lambda_{\max}$. (c) at $\alpha=0.2$ the factor for $\lambda=10$ is $-1$: the mode oscillates forever, so "$\le$" is wrong; also $10/11$ is wrong. (d) $\alpha=1/10$ is admissible but gives factor $0.9$, slower than $9/11\approx0.818$.

I.5 Ch 3 effective modesMCQ

Same $f$ and $Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,4,10)$, now with $\alpha=0.25$ and $x_0=(1,1,0)^T$, in exact arithmetic. What happens?

  1. (a) The iterates converge to $0$: the mode factors are $0.75,\ 0,\ -1.5$, and the third component is $0$ at the start and stays $0$.
  2. (b) The iterates diverge, because $\alpha>2/\lambda_{\max}$.
  3. (c) The iterates reach $0$ in exactly one step.
  4. (d) The iterates oscillate without converging, because $1-4\alpha=0$.
Show answer

Answer: (a). $x_{k+1,i}=(1-\alpha\lambda_i)x_{k,i}$: factors $0.75, 0, -1.5$. The first component decays like $0.75^k$, the second is killed in one step, the third is $0\cdot(-1.5)^k=0$. The range $\alpha<2/\lambda_{\max}$ is for every start; for a start with no component along the bad eigenvector, only the eigenvalues that are present matter (the "effective" spectrum). (b) false for this start (true for a generic start). (c) the first component is $0.75$ after one step. (d) factor 0 means that mode vanishes, not oscillates.

CG (exact arithmetic) on $f=\frac12x^TQx-b^Tx$, where $Q\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$ is SPD with eigenvalues $1,1,1,5,5,9$. From any $x_0$, the minimiser is reached in at most

At the basis $\{x_3,x_4\}$, what are $\lambda$ and the reduced costs $(\mu_1,\mu_2)$, and what follows?

  1. (a) $\lambda=(4,2)$, $\mu_N=(-6,-3)$; not optimal.
  2. (b) $\lambda=0$, $\mu_N=(2,1)$; optimal.

II.6 unboundedness / degeneracyMCQ

For a general standard-form LP at a BFS with basis $B$, suppose some nonbasic $q$ has $\mu_q<0$. Which statement is valid in general?

  1. (a) If some basic variable equals $0$ (degenerate BFS), the step $\theta$ is necessarily positive.
  2. (b) If $B^{-1}a_q\le0$, the dual is unbounded above.
  3. (c) If $B^{-1}a_q\le0$ componentwise, the ray $x+\theta d$ stays feasible for all $\theta\ge0$ and the cost $c^Tx+\theta\mu_q\to-\infty$: the LP is unbounded below (so the dual is infeasible).

Answer: (c). $d_B=-B^{-1}a_q\ge0$, so no basic variable decreases: no ratio, $\theta$ unlimited, cost decreases by $\theta|\mu_q|$. Primal unbounded ⇒ dual infeasible. (a) degenerate BFS can give $\theta=0$ (basis changes, point does not). (b) the primal is unbounded and the dual infeasible, not unbounded. (d) $\mu_q<0$ only says "not optimal; let $q$ enter".

  2. [3] Show that $Q$ is indefinite, but positive definite on $\operatorname{null}(A)$.
  3. [3] Solve the KKT system for $(x^*,\lambda^*)$ and compute $f(x^*)$.
  4. [2] Using a null-space parametrisation of the feasible set, show that $x^*$ is the unique global minimiser.
  5. [6] Active set. For $\min\frac12\|x\|^2-3x_1-2x_2$ s.t. $x_1+x_2\le2$ (1), $-x_1\le0$ (2), $-x_2\le0$ (3), start at $x_0=(0,0)^T$ with $W_0=\{2,3\}$. Constraints are $a_i^Tx\le b_i$ and working-set multipliers satisfy $g_k+\sum_{i\in W_k}\lambda_ia_i=0$. (i) Compute $p_0$ and the multipliers; which constraint is dropped? (ii) After dropping it, compute the new direction $p$. (iii) Do the ratio test and give the accepted step $\min\{1,\alpha_k\}$, the new point and the new working set. (iv) Continue to the optimum and verify optimality by the multiplier signs.

  1. KKT system. $Qx+c+A^T\lambda=0$, $Ax=b$: $$\begin{pmatrix}Q&A^T\\A&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-c\\b\end{pmatrix}:\quad \begin{pmatrix}1&2&0&1\\2&1&0&-1\\0&0&2&0\\1&-1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\x_2\\x_3\\\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\2\\1\end{pmatrix}.$$
  2. Curvature. Eigenvalues of $Q$: $3,-1$ (from the $2\times2$ block) and $2$ → indefinite; e.g. $v=(1,-1,0)$: $v^TQv=1-4+1=-2<0$. Null space: $Ax=0\iff x_1=x_2$, basis $Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, $AZ=0$, $Z^TQZ=\begin{pmatrix}6&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}\succ0$. So $Q$ is PD on null$(A)$ (the bad direction $(1,-1,0)$ is not in null$(A)$).
  3. Solve. Row 3: $x_3=1$. Rows 1+2: $3x_1+3x_2=3\Rightarrow x_1+x_2=1$; with $x_1-x_2=1$: $x_1=1$, $x_2=0$. Row 1: $\lambda=2-1-0=1$. So $x^*=(1,0,1)$, $\lambda^*=1$. $f^*=\frac12(1+0+2)+(-2-2)=\frac32-4=-\frac52$. ($\det K=-12\ne0$.)
  4. Null-space argument. Every feasible point is $x=(1,0,0)+t(1,1,0)+s(0,0,1)=(1+t,t,s)$. Substituting, $\varphi(t,s)=3t^2+s^2-2s-\frac32$, whose Hessian is $Z^TQZ=\operatorname{diag}(6,2)\succ0$: strictly convex, unique minimiser $t=0,s=1$, i.e. $x^*=(1,0,1)$, $\varphi=-\frac52$. So $x^*$ is the unique global minimiser even though $Q$ is indefinite (Q PD on null$(A)$ is enough; Q need not be PD).
  5. Active set. $Q=I$, $c=(-3,-2)$, $a_1=(1,1),b_1=2$; $a_2=(-1,0),b_2=0$; $a_3=(0,-1),b_3=0$.
    • (i) $W_0=\{2,3\}$: two independent normals in $\mathbb{R}^2$ force $p_0=0$. $g_0=(-3,-2)$; $g_0+\lambda_2a_2+\lambda_3a_3=0\Rightarrow\lambda_2=-3$, $\lambda_3=-2$. Negative multipliers ⇒ not optimal; drop the most negative, constraint 2 ($x_1\ge0$).
    • (ii) $W=\{3\}$: minimise $q(p)=g_0^Tp+\frac12\|p\|^2$ s.t. $p_2=0$ ⇒ $p=(3,0)$. Check $g_0^Tp=-9=-p^TQp$ ✓, and $a_2^Tp=-3<0$: it points into the released constraint ✓.
    • (iii) Blocking candidates $i\notin W$ with $a_i^Tp>0$: only (1), $a_1^Tp=3$, ratio $(2-0)/3=\frac23$. $\alpha_k=\frac23<1$ ⇒ step $\min\{1,\frac23\}=\frac23$, $x_1=(2,0)$, $W_1=\{1,3\}$.
    • (iv) At $(2,0)$: $p=0$, $g=(-1,-2)$; $g+\lambda_1(1,1)+\lambda_3(0,-1)=0\Rightarrow\lambda_1=1$, $\lambda_3=-1<0$: drop 3. $W=\{1\}$: $p=t(1,-1)$, $q=(-1+2)t+t^2$ ⇒ $t=-\frac12$, $p=(-\frac12,\frac12)$. Ratio: (2) $a_2^Tp=\frac12>0$, ratio $2/\frac12=4$; (3) $a_3^Tp<0$. $\alpha_k=4>1$ ⇒ full step: $x=(\frac32,\frac12)$. Now $g=(-\frac32,-\frac32)$, $\lambda_1=\frac32\ge0$ ⇒ optimal (all working multipliers $\ge0$, $Q\succeq0$). Check: projection of $(3,2)$ onto $x_1+x_2\le2$ is $(3,2)-\frac32(1,1)=(\frac32,\frac12)$ ✓.

Solutions notes: how to score

Which five to pick
  • Always take IV (ADMM). He promised a "hard problem" on ADMM and said he would ask you to derive each step's exact formula. The recipe is fixed: split → $L_A$ → complete the square → linear system / soft threshold / clip → $\lambda+\beta\times$residual. Even a partial answer earns most of (b), (c), (e).
  • Take V (KKT ladder) if you can write all five KKT parts and the critical cone cold; it is in every constrained paper ("seventy percent of mark").
  • Then one of VI / VII: VI if LP duals are your strength (fast, mechanical), VII if you are comfortable with $Z^TQZ$ and the active-set ratio test.
  • Fill the last two slots with MCQ groups you can finish in 20 minutes each: III (theorem facts + weak duality chain) is usually the fastest; then I or II. MCQ groups are quick marks but one sign slip costs 2 marks each, so read every option.
  • A safe pattern: IV + V + VI + III + one of I/II. If short of time, it is better to finish four questions well than five badly — but the paper requires five, so leave 15 minutes for the fifth.
Self-marking checklist
  • Part A: give the 2 marks only if every sign and every part is right (e.g. KKT with all five parts, $\mu\ge0$, CS "$=0$").
  • IV: ½ vs no-½ in the data term changes the linear system; the $x_2$-step centre is $Dx_1+\lambda/\beta$ (plus sign); threshold $\gamma/\beta$.
  • V: critical cone at a degenerate point ($\mu=0$, active) is a half-space, not a line; SOSC failing at $a=0$ does not mean "not a minimiser".
  • VI: free variable ⇒ equality in the dual; inner infimum over all $x$.
  • VII: $Q$ need not be PD; ratio test only over $a_i^Tp>0$; accepted step $\min\{1,\alpha_k\}$; drop a constraint with negative multiplier.
