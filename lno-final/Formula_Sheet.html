M1 · ADMM (Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers) open module →

Memorise cold
  • Problem: $\min_{x_1,x_2}\ f_1(x_1)+f_2(x_2)$ s.t. $A(x_1,x_2)=A_1x_1+A_2x_2-b=0$.
  • Augmented Lagrangian ($\beta>0$ fixed): $$L_A(x_1,x_2,\lambda)=f_1(x_1)+f_2(x_2)+\lambda^T A(x_1,x_2)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|A(x_1,x_2)\|_2^2$$
  • One ADMM iteration (order matters): $$\begin{aligned} x_1^{(k+1)}&\in\arg\min_{x_1} L_A(x_1,\,x_2^{(k)},\,\lambda^{(k)})\\ x_2^{(k+1)}&\in\arg\min_{x_2} L_A(x_1^{(k+1)},\,x_2,\,\lambda^{(k)})\\ \lambda^{(k+1)}&=\lambda^{(k)}+\beta\,A(x_1^{(k+1)},x_2^{(k+1)}) \end{aligned}$$ The $x_2$-step uses the new $x_1$ and the old $\lambda$.
  • Completing the square (the one identity you use every time): $$\lambda^T(z-c)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-c\|^2=\frac{\beta}{2}\Big\|z-\big(c-\tfrac{\lambda}{\beta}\big)\Big\|^2-\frac{\|\lambda\|^2}{2\beta}$$ If the sign inside is flipped ($\lambda^T(c-z)$), the centre is $c+\lambda/\beta$.
  • Soft thresholding: $\arg\min_z\ \gamma|z|+\frac{\beta}{2}(z-y)^2=S_{\gamma/\beta}(y)$, with $S_\tau(t)=\operatorname{sign}(t)\max(|t|-\tau,0)$, i.e. $t-\tau$ if $t>\tau$; $0$ if $|t|\le\tau$; $t+\tau$ if $t<-\tau$.
  • Indicator → projection: $\arg\min_v\ P_C(v)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|v-y\|^2=\Pi_C(y)$. Box $[0,B]^n$: clip each component, $\min(\max(y_i,0),B)$. Orthant: $\max(y_i,0)$.
  • LASSO answer ($\min \frac12\|Mx-b\|^2+\gamma\|x\|_1$, split $x_1-x_2=0$): $$\begin{aligned} (M^TM+\beta I)\,x_1^{(k+1)}&=M^Tb+\beta x_2^{(k)}-\lambda^{(k)}\\ x_2^{(k+1)}&=S_{\gamma/\beta}\big(x_1^{(k+1)}+\lambda^{(k)}/\beta\big)\\ \lambda^{(k+1)}&=\lambda^{(k)}+\beta\,(x_1^{(k+1)}-x_2^{(k+1)}) \end{aligned}$$

M2 · Constrained optimality: KKT, cones, second order open module →

Memorise cold (course form, Theorem 6.3.3 and 6.4.1–6.4.2)
  • Problem form: $\min f(x)$ s.t. $h_i(x)=0$ ($i=1..m$), $g_j(x)\le 0$ ($j=1..p$). Rewrite every constraint into this form first: $x_1\ge 0 \to -x_1\le 0$; $x^\top Qx\ge 1\to 1-x^\top Qx\le 0$. For a max, minimise $-F$.
  • Lagrangian: $\ell(x,\lambda,\mu)=f(x)+\sum_i\lambda_i h_i(x)+\sum_j \mu_j g_j(x)$ (plus signs). Matrix form of stationarity: $\nabla f(x^\star)+J_h(x^\star)\lambda^\star+J_g(x^\star)\mu^\star=0$, with $J$ = columns are gradients.
  • KKT (five parts): (1) stationarity $\nabla_x\ell=0$; (2) $h(x^\star)=0$; (3) $g(x^\star)\le 0$; (4) $\mu^\star\ge 0$; (5) $\mu_j^\star g_j(x^\star)=0$ for every $j$. $\lambda$ has no sign.
  • KKT is necessary only at regular points (LICQ: gradients of all equalities + active inequalities linearly independent). Under LICQ the multipliers are unique.
  • Active split: $\mathcal A_+=\{j\in\mathcal A:\mu_j^\star\gt 0\}$, $\mathcal A_0=\{j\in\mathcal A:\mu_j^\star=0\}$.
  • Critical cone: $C=\{d:\ \nabla h_i^\top d=0\ \forall i,\ \ \nabla g_j^\top d=0\ (j\in\mathcal A_+),\ \ \nabla g_j^\top d\le 0\ (j\in\mathcal A_0)\}$. Equivalently $C=\{d\in\mathcal L_\Omega:\nabla f(x^\star)^\top d=0\}$.
  • $L_\star=\nabla^2_{xx}\ell(x^\star,\lambda^\star,\mu^\star)$ = $\nabla^2 f+\sum\lambda_i^\star\nabla^2h_i+\sum\mu_j^\star\nabla^2 g_j$ (multipliers held fixed).
  • SONC (needs regular + local min): $d^\top L_\star d\ge 0$ for every $d\in C$. Fails ⇒ not a local min.
  • SOSC (needs only KKT, no LICQ): $d^\top L_\star d\gt 0$ for every $0\ne d\in C$ ⇒ strict local min. $C=\{0\}$ ⇒ SOSC passes (nothing to test).
  • Cones: $T_\Omega\subseteq\mathcal L_\Omega$ always; regular ⇒ $T_\Omega=\mathcal L_\Omega$; LICQ is sufficient, not necessary; all constraints linear ⇒ $T=\mathcal L$. Geometric FONC (no CQ): $\nabla f(x^\star)^\top d\ge 0$ for all $d\in T_\Omega(x^\star)$.
  • Farkas: [$c^\top d\ge 0$ whenever $A^\top d=0,\ B^\top d\le 0$] ⇔ [$c+A\lambda+B\mu=0$ for some $\lambda$ free, $\mu\ge 0$].
  • Eigenvector answer: $\max x^\top x$ s.t. $x^\top Qx\le 1$ → $1/\lambda_1$ at $\pm v_1/\sqrt{\lambda_1}$; $\min x^\top x$ s.t. $x^\top Qx\ge 1$ (or $=1$) → $1/\lambda_n$ at $\pm v_n/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$.

M3 · Convexity and duality (incl. LP duality) open module →

Memorise cold
  • Primal (always rewrite into this form first): $\min_x f(x)$ s.t. $h(x)=0$, $g(x)\le 0$. Lagrangian $L(x,\lambda,\mu)=f(x)+\lambda^Th(x)+\mu^Tg(x)$, $\lambda$ free, $\mu\ge 0$. (The notes write the multipliers as $y=(u,v)$; same thing.)
  • Dual function $q(\lambda,\mu)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^n}L(x,\lambda,\mu)$: infimum over all $x$, constraints are not put back. Domain $\mathbb{D}=\{(\lambda,\mu):q\gt-\infty\}$. Dual problem: $\max q(\lambda,\mu)$ s.t. $(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{D}$, $\mu\ge0$.
  • Linear-in-$x$ rule: $\inf_x (r^Tx+\beta)=\beta$ if $r=0$, and $-\infty$ otherwise. ("The infimum is finite if and only if the coefficient of $x$ vanishes.")
  • $q$ is concave and $\mathbb{D}$ is convex, even when the primal is nonconvex (because $L$ is affine in the multipliers).
  • Weak duality: $x$ feasible, $\mu\ge0$ ⇒ $q(\lambda,\mu)\le L(x,\lambda,\mu)\le f(x)$. Hence $d^*\le p^*$.
  • Standard LP pair: $\ \min c^Tx$ s.t. $Ax=b,\ x\ge0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \max b^T\lambda$ s.t. $A^T\lambda\le c$ ($\lambda$ free).
  • LP KKT: $A^T\lambda^*+\mu^*=c,\ \ \mu^*\ge0,\ \ Ax^*=b,\ \ x^*\ge0,\ \ (\mu^*)^Tx^*=0$. Value identity $c^Tx^*=b^T\lambda^*$.
  • LP duality theorem: (i) one has an optimal solution ⇒ both do, equal values. (ii) one unbounded ⇒ the other infeasible. The converse of (ii) is FALSE: both can be infeasible.
  • KKT-equivalence (Thm 7.4.1), differentiable convex, full space: a KKT pair exists ⇔ $p^*=d^*\in\mathbb{R}$ and both are attained. Then every primal optimum with every dual optimum is a KKT pair.
  • Slater (Thm 7.4.3): $f,g$ convex, $h$ affine, some $x^0$ with $h(x^0)=0$, $g(x^0)\lt 0$, and $p^*$ finite ⇒ $p^*=d^*$ and the dual optimum is attained. Primal attainment is NOT guaranteed.
  • Primal recovery (Cor 7.4.2): KKT pair exists, $\bar y$ dual optimal, $L(\cdot,\bar y)$ has a unique minimiser $\bar x$ ⇒ $\bar x$ is the unique primal optimum (feasibility and complementary slackness come free). Uniqueness, not strict convexity, is the hypothesis.
  • Convex feasible set from: $h(x)=0$ with $h$ affine; $g(x)\le0$ with $g$ convex; $g(x)\ge0$ with $g$ concave.
  • Never write "concave optimization problem" or "concave set".

M4 · LP (simplex concepts) and QP open module →

Memorise cold: Chapter 8 (LP, standard form $\min c^Tx,\ Ax=b,\ x\ge 0$, rank $A=m$)
  • LP KKT (Lagrangian $c^Tx+\lambda^T(b-Ax)-\mu^Tx$): $\;A^T\lambda+\mu=c,\quad Ax=b,\ x\ge0,\quad \mu\ge0,\quad \mu^Tx=0.$ ($\lambda$ free.)
  • Basis $A=[B\ N]$: $\bar x_B=B^{-1}b,\ \bar x_N=0$ (feasible iff $B^{-1}b\ge0$) · $\bar\lambda=B^{-T}c_B$ · $\bar\mu_B=0$ · $\bar\mu_N=c_N-N^T\bar\lambda$ (reduced costs).
  • Optimal if $\bar x_B\ge0$ and $\bar\mu_N\ge0$. Gap identity: $c^Tx'-c^T\bar x=\bar\mu_N^Tx'_N$ for every feasible $x'$.
  • Step: pick $q$ with $\bar\mu_q\lt0$; $u=B^{-1}a_q$; direction $d_B=-u,\ d_q=1$ (so $Ad=0$, $c^Td=\bar\mu_q$).
  • Ratio test: $\theta=\min_{i:\,u_i\gt0}(\bar x_B)_i/u_i$; cost change $=\theta\bar\mu_q$. Unbounded if $u\le0$ and $\bar\mu_q\lt0$. Degenerate ($\theta=0$ possible) iff some basic variable $=0$.
  • Theorems: extreme point $\Leftrightarrow$ BFS; feasible $\Rightarrow$ a BFS exists; optimum exists $\Rightarrow$ an optimal BFS exists. $\bar x$ is a BFS iff its positive-support columns are independent.
  • Bland: enter = smallest index with $\bar\mu_j\lt0$; leave = smallest original index among ratio ties. New $B'$ nonsingular since $\det B'=u_p\det B$, $u_p\gt0$.
Memorise cold: Chapter 9 (QP $f=\tfrac12x^TQx-c^Tx$)
  • Equality QP KKT ($\ell=f+\lambda^T(Ax-b)$): $\;Qx-c+A^T\lambda=0,\ Ax=b$, i.e. $\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}Q&A^T\\A&0\end{bmatrix}}_{K}\begin{bmatrix}x\\\lambda\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}c\\b\end{bmatrix}.$
  • Unique global minimiser $\iff H_r=Z^TQZ\succ0$ $\iff d^TQd\gt0$ for all $0\ne d\in\ker A$. Then $K$ is nonsingular. $Q$ need not be PD. $K$ nonsingular $\iff H_r$ nonsingular (not PD!).
  • Null-space: $x=x_p+Zz$, $\ \phi(z)=f(x_p)+g_r^Tz+\tfrac12z^TH_rz$, $g_r=Z^T(Qx_p-c)$, solve $H_rz^*=-g_r$.
  • Range-space (Schur), $Q$ nonsingular: $S=AQ^{-1}A^T$, $\ S\lambda=AQ^{-1}c-b$, $\ x=Q^{-1}(c-A^T\lambda)$. $Q\succ0\Rightarrow S\succ0$.
  • Projector: $P=I-A^T(AA^T)^{-1}A=Z(Z^TZ)^{-1}Z^T$; $r=-P\nabla f$ gives $\nabla f^Tr=-\|r\|^2$. Projected CG: at most $n-m$ steps.
  • Active set: $g_k=Qx_k-c$, $\ q_k(p)=g_k^Tp+\tfrac12p^TQp$ on $a_i^Tp=0\ (i\in W_k)$. If $p_k\ne0$: $g_k^Tp_k=-p_k^TQp_k\lt0$, $\ f(x_k+\alpha p_k)-f(x_k)=-\alpha(1-\tfrac\alpha2)p_k^TQp_k$.
  • Ratio test: $\alpha_k=\min_{i\notin W_k,\ a_i^Tp_k\gt0}\dfrac{b_i-a_i^Tx_k}{a_i^Tp_k}$; step $=\min\{1,\alpha_k\}$; if $\alpha_k\le1$ add the blocker. If $p_k=0$: solve $g_k+\sum_{W_k}\lambda_ia_i=0$; all $\lambda_i\ge0\Rightarrow$ stop (global min if $Q\succeq0$); else drop the most negative.
Sign conventions differ between the chapters Ch 8 uses $\lambda^T(b-Ax)$ so stationarity is $c=A^T\lambda+\mu$. Ch 9 uses $\lambda^T(Ax-b)$ so stationarity is $Qx-c+A^T\lambda=0$. Use the one the question states; if none is stated, say which you use.

M5 · Unconstrained algorithms: line search, GD, Newton, CG open module →

Memorise cold: line search (Ch 2)
  • Descent direction: $\nabla f(x_k)^Td_k\lt0$. Exact step on a quadratic along any $d_k$: $\alpha_k=-\dfrac{g_k^Td_k}{d_k^TQd_k}$; for steepest descent $d_k=-g_k$: $\alpha_k=\dfrac{g_k^Tg_k}{g_k^TQg_k}$.
  • Write $\phi(\alpha)=f(x_k+\alpha d_k)$, so $\phi'(0)=\nabla f(x_k)^Td_k\lt0$. With $0\lt c_1\lt c_2\lt1$:
    Armijo (sufficient decrease): $\phi(\alpha)\le\phi(0)+c_1\alpha\phi'(0)$.
    Wolfe = Armijo + curvature $\phi'(\alpha)\ge c_2\phi'(0)$.
    Strong Wolfe = Armijo + $|\phi'(\alpha)|\le c_2|\phi'(0)|$.
    Goldstein ($0\lt c\lt\tfrac12$): $\phi(0)+(1-c)\alpha\phi'(0)\le\phi(\alpha)\le\phi(0)+c\,\alpha\phi'(0)$.
    Backtracking: try $\alpha=1,s,s^2,\dots$ ($0\lt s\lt1$), accept the first that passes Armijo.
  • Golden section: interior points at fraction $\rho=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx0.382$ from each end; the interval shrinks by $1-\rho=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\approx0.618$ per new evaluation (one old point is reused).
  • 1-D Newton: $x_{k+1}=x_k-f'(x_k)/f''(x_k)$. Needs $f''\gt0$, and can still fail far away.
  • Zoutendijk: strong Wolfe + $f$ bounded below + $\nabla f$ Lipschitz ⇒ $\sum_k\cos^2\theta_k\|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2\lt\infty$, $\cos\theta_k=\frac{-\nabla f_k^Td_k}{\|\nabla f_k\|\|d_k\|}$. If $\cos\theta_k\ge\delta\gt0$ then $\|\nabla f(x_k)\|\to0$. $d_k=-D_k\nabla f_k$ with eig$(D_k)\in[m,M]$ gives $\cos\theta_k\ge m/M$.
Memorise cold: gradient descent and Newton (Ch 3)
  • Fixed step: $e_{k+1}=(I-\alpha Q)e_k$; eigen-mode $i$ is multiplied by $(1-\alpha\lambda_i)$ each step.
  • Converges from every $x_0$ ⇔ $|1-\alpha\lambda_i|\lt1\ \forall i$ ⇔ $\boxed{0\lt\alpha\lt 2/\lambda_{\max}}$. Best $\alpha=\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max}}$, worst-case factor $\dfrac{\kappa-1}{\kappa+1}$ on $\|e_k\|$.
  • Multiplier $=-1$ ⇒ that mode flips sign forever (2-cycle). $|{\cdot}|\gt1$ ⇒ diverges, unless $e_0$ has zero component in that eigen-direction (then converges along the other eigen-lines only).
  • Exact line search SD: $f(x_{k+1})-f^*\le\left(\dfrac{\kappa-1}{\kappa+1}\right)^2(f(x_k)-f^*)$ (Kantorovich). Consecutive gradients are orthogonal ⇒ zigzag. If $g_0$ lives only in some eigen-directions, use the effective $\kappa=\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$ over those directions only.
  • A gradient step with $\eta=1/\lambda$ kills the $\lambda$-component exactly ($1-\lambda/\lambda=0$).
  • Newton: $H_kp_k=-g_k$, $x_{k+1}=x_k+p_k$. One step on a quadratic with $Q\succ0$ lands on $x^*$. Quadratic convergence $\|e_{k+1}\|\le C\|e_k\|^2$ only for $x_0$ sufficiently close. Descent iff $H_k\succ0$ ($g_k^Tp_k=-p_k^TH_kp_k$). Damped Newton + Armijo ($c_1\lt\frac12$, start at $\alpha=1$) eventually accepts $\alpha=1$.
Memorise cold: conjugate directions / CG (Ch 4)
  • Affine-space minimum ($f$ convex): $x_S$ minimises $f$ on $\bar x+\mathrm{range}(D)$ ⇔ $D^T\nabla f(x_S)=0$. The gradient of $h(z)=f(\bar x+Dz)$ is zero; the gradient of $f$ is only orthogonal to every $d_i$, not zero.
  • Q-conjugate: $d_i^TQd_j=0$ ($i\ne j$). Nonzero Q-conjugate vectors are linearly independent (so at most $n$). Eigenvectors of $Q$ are both orthogonal and Q-conjugate.
  • Key formula: $g_i=g_0+\sum_{j\lt i}\alpha_jQd_j$. With conjugate directions: $d_i^Tg_i=d_i^Tg_0$, and $g_k\perp d_0,\dots,d_{k-1}$.
  • CG: $d_0=-g_0$; $\ \alpha_k=\dfrac{\|g_k\|^2}{d_k^TQd_k}$; $\ x_{k+1}=x_k+\alpha_kd_k$; $\ g_{k+1}=g_k+\alpha_kQd_k$; $\ \beta_k=\dfrac{\|g_{k+1}\|^2}{\|g_k\|^2}$; $\ d_{k+1}=-g_{k+1}+\beta_kd_k$.
  • $g_k^Td_k=-\|g_k\|^2$. Gradients orthogonal ($g_i^Tg_j=0$), directions Q-conjugate ($d_i^TQd_j=0$). Never swap.
  • $x_k$ minimises $f$ over $x_0+\mathrm{span}\{g_0,Qg_0,\dots,Q^{k-1}g_0\}$ (highest power $k-1$).
  • CG steps to reach $x^*$ = number of distinct eigenvalues whose eigenspaces $e_0$ actually touches ($\le n$).
  • Decrease per step: $f(x_k)-f(x_{k+1})=\dfrac{\|g_k\|^4}{2\,d_k^TQd_k}$ (the ½ is on $f$; for $E=\|x-x^*\|_Q^2$ the decrease is twice that).
  • PCG ($B\approx Q^{-1}$, SPD): $d_0=-Bg_0$, $\alpha_k=\dfrac{g_k^TBg_k}{d_k^TQd_k}$, $\beta_k=\dfrac{g_{k+1}^TBg_{k+1}}{g_k^TBg_k}$, $d_{k+1}=-Bg_{k+1}+\beta_kd_k$. Then $g_i^TBg_j=0$, $d_i^TQd_j=0$, $g_k^Td_k=-g_k^TBg_k$. Replacing $B$ by $cB$ changes nothing in $x_k$. Speed depends on eig$(B^{1/2}QB^{1/2})$.
  • Non-quadratic CG, four changes: (1) $\beta$ back to the original formula with $Q\to F(x_k)=\nabla^2f(x_k)$; (2) set $\beta_k=0$ if $d_k^TF(x_k)d_k\le0$; (3) $\alpha_k$ by a line search (no closed form); (4) check $g_k^Td_k\lt0$, else reset $d_k=-g_k$. FR + strong Wolfe ($c_2\lt\frac12$): $-\frac{1}{1-c_2}\le\frac{g_k^Td_k}{\|g_k\|^2}\le\frac{2c_2-1}{1-c_2}\lt0$.

M6 · Unconstrained optimality and convexity open module →

Memorise cold
  • FONC (necessary): $x^*$ local min, $f$ differentiable $\Rightarrow \nabla f(x^*) = 0$.
  • SONC (necessary): $x^*$ local min, $f \in C^2$ $\Rightarrow \nabla f(x^*) = 0$ and $\nabla^2 f(x^*) \succeq 0$ (PSD).
  • SOSC (sufficient): $\nabla f(x^*) = 0$ and $\nabla^2 f(x^*) \succ 0$ (PD) $\Rightarrow x^*$ is a strict (strong) local min.
  • The gaps: $x^4$ (strict min, $f''=0$: PD is not necessary); $x^3$ and $-x^4$ (FONC+SONC hold, not a min: PSD is not sufficient).
  • Quadratics $f = \tfrac12 x^\top Q x - b^\top x + r$: $\nabla f = Qx - b$, $\nabla^2 f = Q$. Here "both directions" hold: $\{\nabla f = 0,\ Q\succeq 0\}$ ⇔ (global) min; $\{\nabla f = 0,\ Q \succ 0\}$ ⇔ strict (unique) min. $Q$ has a negative eigenvalue ⇒ unbounded below. $Q \succeq 0$ singular: $b\in\text{range}(Q)$ ⇒ infinitely many minimisers; $b\notin\text{range}(Q)$ ⇒ none.
  • Directional derivatives: $\frac{d}{dt}f(x+td)\big|_{0} = \nabla f(x)^\top d$, $\ \frac{d^2}{dt^2}f(x+td)\big|_{0} = d^\top \nabla^2 f(x)\, d$.
  • PD / PSD tests (symmetric $A$): PD ⇔ all eigenvalues $\gt 0$ ⇔ all leading principal minors $\gt 0$ (Sylvester). PSD ⇔ all eigenvalues $\ge 0$ ⇔ all principal minors $\ge 0$ (leading ones are not enough).
  • Least squares $\tfrac12\|Ax-\bar b\|^2$: normal equations $A^\top A x^* = A^\top \bar b$ (always solvable, since range$(A^\top A)$ = range$(A^\top)$). Unique ⇔ $A$ has full column rank; then $x^* = (A^\top A)^{-1}A^\top \bar b$, projector $P = A(A^\top A)^{-1}A^\top$ ($P^2=P$, $P^\top=P$), projector onto null$(A^\top)$ is $I-P$.
  • Convexity ($f\in C^2$ on $\mathbb R^n$): definition $f(\theta x+(1-\theta)y)\le\theta f(x)+(1-\theta)f(y)$ ⇔ $f(y)\ge f(x)+\nabla f(x)^\top(y-x)$ for all $x,y$ ⇔ $\nabla^2 f(x)\succeq 0$ for every $x$ ⇔ every restriction to a line is convex. Convex: local min = global min; stationary point = global min. Max of convex functions is convex.
  • Peano-type: $f(y,z) = (z-py^2)(z-qy^2)$, $0\lt p\lt q$: min at 0 along every line, but $f(y,my^2)\lt 0$ for $p\lt m\lt q$ ⇒ not a local min.
  • Weierstrass: $X$ nonempty closed, $f$ proper, lsc, coercive ($\|x\|\to\infty \Rightarrow f(x)\to+\infty$), $X\cap\text{dom} f\ne\emptyset$ ⇒ a minimiser exists. $r(h) = o(h^2)$ means $r(h)/h^2 \to 0$.

Trap checklist (read the night before and in the morning)

Signs and forms
  • Rewrite constraints as $g\le0$, $h=0$ first; $L=f+\lambda^Th+\mu^Tg$, $\mu\ge0$, $\lambda$ free. Write all five KKT parts.
  • $B^{-1}$ vs $B^{-T}$: $x_B=B^{-1}b$, $\lambda=B^{-T}c_B$. Ch 8 and Ch 9 write the LP/QP Lagrangian with opposite signs; say which you use.
  • Energy/objective factor ½; ADMM data term with or without ½ changes the linear system.
Logic
  • Necessary vs sufficient; strict vs non-strict; local vs global; PSD vs PD.
  • SONC/SOSC are checked on the critical cone, not all of $\mathbb{R}^n$; an indefinite full Hessian can still pass SOSC; $C=\{0\}$ ⇒ SOSC holds. SOSC gives a strict local min.
  • KKT is necessary only at regular points (LICQ); sufficiency needs no LICQ. Active constraint does not imply $\mu>0$.
  • Convex problem: KKT ⇒ global min; KKT still not necessary without a CQ. Never say "concave optimization problem".
Duality
  • Dual function: infimum over all $x$ (constraints are not put back); find where $q=-\infty$.
  • Slater ⇒ zero gap + dual attainment (not primal). Primal recovery needs a unique Lagrangian minimiser (strict convexity is one sufficient condition).
  • LP: one optimal ⇒ both optimal with equal values; unbounded ⇒ other infeasible; infeasible ⇏ unbounded.
Algorithms
  • CG: gradients mutually orthogonal, directions mutually $Q$-conjugate (don't swap). Krylov span up to power $k-1$. PCG: $g_i^TBg_j=0$; $B\approx Q^{-1}$.
  • GD on a quadratic: $0<\alpha<2/\lambda_{\max}$; best $\alpha=2/(\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max})$.
  • Simplex: degenerate BFS can give $\theta=0$. QP: $Q$ PD on $\ker A$ is enough; $Q$ need not be PD. Active-set step is $\min\{1,\alpha_k\}$.
  • ADMM: $x_2$-step uses new $x_1$, old $\lambda$; threshold is $\gamma/\beta$; projection onto a box = clip.
