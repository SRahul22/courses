M2 · Constrained optimality: KKT, cones, second order

Chapter 6 · about 90 min (Must remember 5 · Concepts 35 · What he said 3 · Practice 40 · Self-test 7) · Goal: given any smooth constrained problem, run the full KKT ladder (rewrite → Lagrangian → KKT → active sets → multipliers → LICQ → critical cone → SONC/SOSC → classify) without a single sign error, and answer every cone/LICQ/second-order trap MCQ.

1. Must remember

Memorise cold (course form, Theorem 6.3.3 and 6.4.1–6.4.2)
  • Problem form: $\min f(x)$ s.t. $h_i(x)=0$ ($i=1..m$), $g_j(x)\le 0$ ($j=1..p$). Rewrite every constraint into this form first: $x_1\ge 0 \to -x_1\le 0$; $x^\top Qx\ge 1\to 1-x^\top Qx\le 0$. For a max, minimise $-F$.
  • Lagrangian: $\ell(x,\lambda,\mu)=f(x)+\sum_i\lambda_i h_i(x)+\sum_j \mu_j g_j(x)$ (plus signs). Matrix form of stationarity: $\nabla f(x^\star)+J_h(x^\star)\lambda^\star+J_g(x^\star)\mu^\star=0$, with $J$ = columns are gradients.
  • KKT (five parts): (1) stationarity $\nabla_x\ell=0$; (2) $h(x^\star)=0$; (3) $g(x^\star)\le 0$; (4) $\mu^\star\ge 0$; (5) $\mu_j^\star g_j(x^\star)=0$ for every $j$. $\lambda$ has no sign.
  • KKT is necessary only at regular points (LICQ: gradients of all equalities + active inequalities linearly independent). Under LICQ the multipliers are unique.
  • Active split: $\mathcal A_+=\{j\in\mathcal A:\mu_j^\star\gt 0\}$, $\mathcal A_0=\{j\in\mathcal A:\mu_j^\star=0\}$.
  • Critical cone: $C=\{d:\ \nabla h_i^\top d=0\ \forall i,\ \ \nabla g_j^\top d=0\ (j\in\mathcal A_+),\ \ \nabla g_j^\top d\le 0\ (j\in\mathcal A_0)\}$. Equivalently $C=\{d\in\mathcal L_\Omega:\nabla f(x^\star)^\top d=0\}$.
  • $L_\star=\nabla^2_{xx}\ell(x^\star,\lambda^\star,\mu^\star)$ = $\nabla^2 f+\sum\lambda_i^\star\nabla^2h_i+\sum\mu_j^\star\nabla^2 g_j$ (multipliers held fixed).
  • SONC (needs regular + local min): $d^\top L_\star d\ge 0$ for every $d\in C$. Fails ⇒ not a local min.
  • SOSC (needs only KKT, no LICQ): $d^\top L_\star d\gt 0$ for every $0\ne d\in C$ ⇒ strict local min. $C=\{0\}$ ⇒ SOSC passes (nothing to test).
  • Cones: $T_\Omega\subseteq\mathcal L_\Omega$ always; regular ⇒ $T_\Omega=\mathcal L_\Omega$; LICQ is sufficient, not necessary; all constraints linear ⇒ $T=\mathcal L$. Geometric FONC (no CQ): $\nabla f(x^\star)^\top d\ge 0$ for all $d\in T_\Omega(x^\star)$.
  • Farkas: [$c^\top d\ge 0$ whenever $A^\top d=0,\ B^\top d\le 0$] ⇔ [$c+A\lambda+B\mu=0$ for some $\lambda$ free, $\mu\ge 0$].
  • Eigenvector answer: $\max x^\top x$ s.t. $x^\top Qx\le 1$ → $1/\lambda_1$ at $\pm v_1/\sqrt{\lambda_1}$; $\min x^\top x$ s.t. $x^\top Qx\ge 1$ (or $=1$) → $1/\lambda_n$ at $\pm v_n/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$.

2. Concepts

2.1 Setup: rewrite, then build the Lagrangian

In plain words: the professor's whole sign system rests on one convention: every inequality is written as "something $\le 0$". If you write it the other way, the sign of $\mu$ flips and you will reject the right answer. So step zero of every problem is: rewrite the constraints, give each one a name, then write $\ell$.

Precisely: $\Omega=\{x: h_i(x)=0,\ i=1..m;\ g_j(x)\le 0,\ j=1..p\}$; active set $\mathcal A(x^\star)=\{j:g_j(x^\star)=0\}$; $\ell=f+\lambda^\top h+\mu^\top g$. (The notes write $\ell$ for the Lagrangian and $L_\star$ for its Hessian; Midterm 2 wrote $L$ for the Lagrangian. Same thing; read the notation in the question.)

Worked example Problem: $\max\ x_1x_2$ s.t. $x_1+x_2\ge 1$, $x_1^2+x_2^2=4$, $x_2\le 3$.
  1. Max → min: $f(x)=-x_1x_2$.
  2. Equality: $h(x)=x_1^2+x_2^2-4=0$.
  3. Inequalities: $g_1(x)=1-x_1-x_2\le 0$, $g_2(x)=x_2-3\le 0$.
  4. $\ell=-x_1x_2+\lambda(x_1^2+x_2^2-4)+\mu_1(1-x_1-x_2)+\mu_2(x_2-3)$, $\lambda$ free, $\mu_1,\mu_2\ge 0$.
Trap Writing $g=x_1+x_2-1\ge 0$ and then $\ell=f+\mu g$ with $\mu\ge 0$ gives the wrong sign. He said: other books' forms "not accepted". Always "$\le 0$" and "+".

2.2 The five KKT parts

In plain words: at a (regular) minimum, the downhill direction $-\nabla f$ must be "pushed back" by the constraints: it is a combination of equality gradients (any sign: an equality can push either way) and active inequality gradients with nonnegative weights (a wall can only push you back inside, never pull you out). Inactive constraints get weight zero; complementary slackness is just the bookkeeping that says so without naming the active set.

Precisely (Theorem 6.3.3, KKT necessary conditions): if $f,h,g$ are $C^1$ near a local minimizer $x^\star$ and $x^\star$ is regular, then there exist $\lambda^\star\in\mathbb R^m$, $\mu^\star\in\mathbb R^p$ with

$$\underbrace{\nabla f(x^\star)+\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i^\star\nabla h_i(x^\star)+\sum_{j=1}^p\mu_j^\star\nabla g_j(x^\star)=0}_{\text{stationarity}},\quad \underbrace{h(x^\star)=0,\ g(x^\star)\le 0}_{\text{primal feasibility}},\quad \underbrace{\mu^\star\ge 0}_{\text{dual feasibility}},\quad \underbrace{\mu_j^\star g_j(x^\star)=0\ \forall j}_{\text{complementarity}}.$$

Facts: $\mu_j\gt 0\Rightarrow g_j=0$ (active); $g_j\lt 0\Rightarrow\mu_j=0$; but active does not imply $\mu_j\gt 0$ (degenerate active constraint). Because every product $\mu_jg_j\le 0$, the single equation $\mu^\top g=0$ is equivalent to all $p$ products being zero (no cancellation).

Worked example (Quiz 3 Q14) $\min (x-2)^2$ s.t. $g(x)=x-1\le 0$. $\ell=(x-2)^2+\mu(x-1)$.
  1. KKT: $2(x-2)+\mu=0$, $x\le 1$, $\mu\ge 0$, $\mu(x-1)=0$.
  2. Inactive ($\mu=0$): $x=2$, infeasible. Reject.
  3. Active ($x=1$): $\mu=2\ge 0$. KKT pair $(x^\star,\mu^\star)=(1,2)$.
  4. $\mu\gt 0$ so $C=\{d: 1\cdot d=0\}=\{0\}$ ⇒ SOSC passes vacuously ⇒ strict local min (in fact global, convex problem).

2.3 The KKT ladder: the procedure for every descriptive question

The ladder (write every rung)
  1. Rewrite constraints as $h=0$, $g\le 0$; max → min of $-F$.
  2. Lagrangian $\ell=f+\lambda^\top h+\mu^\top g$.
  3. Write all five KKT parts explicitly (he marks this).
  4. Enumerate active sets $\mathcal A\subseteq\{1..p\}$: set $\mu_j=0$ for $j\notin\mathcal A$, $g_j=0$ for $j\in\mathcal A$.
  5. Solve stationarity + $h=0$ + active $g_j=0$ for $x,\lambda,\mu_{\mathcal A}$.
  6. Check $\mu_{\mathcal A}\ge 0$ and that inactive $g_j(x)\lt 0$ (or $\le 0$) and $h=0$. Discard failures, saying which condition fails.
  7. LICQ at each survivor (also ask: could a non-regular feasible point be a minimum the KKT search missed?).
  8. Split active set into $\mathcal A_+$ ($\mu\gt 0$) and $\mathcal A_0$ ($\mu=0$).
  9. Critical cone $C$: equalities for $h$ and $\mathcal A_+$, "$\le$" for $\mathcal A_0$.
  10. Hessian of $\ell$ with multipliers fixed: $L_\star$.
  11. Test $d^\top L_\star d$ on $C$: $\gt 0$ for all $d\ne 0$ ⇒ SOSC; some $d\in C$ with $\lt 0$ at a regular point ⇒ SONC fails; $\ge 0$ with some zero ⇒ inconclusive. $C=\{0\}$ ⇒ SOSC passes.
  12. Classify each point in one clear sentence (strict local min / not a local min / undecided), then global (compare values; existence via compactness or coercivity).
Worked example: full ladder (Quiz 3 Q13) $\min (u-2)^2+v^2$ s.t. $u+v=1$, $u\ge 0$, $v\ge 0$.
  1. $h=u+v-1=0$, $g_1=-u\le 0$, $g_2=-v\le 0$. $\ell=(u-2)^2+v^2+\lambda(u+v-1)-\mu_1u-\mu_2v$.
  2. KKT: $2(u-2)+\lambda-\mu_1=0$, $2v+\lambda-\mu_2=0$; $u+v=1$; $u,v\ge 0$; $\mu_1,\mu_2\ge 0$; $\mu_1u=0$, $\mu_2v=0$.
  3. $\mathcal A=\varnothing$: $\mu=0$ ⇒ $2(u-2)=2v$ with $u+v=1$ ⇒ $(u,v)=(3/2,-1/2)$, $\lambda=1$: violates $v\ge 0$. Reject.
  4. $\mathcal A=\{1\}$: $u=0,v=1$, $\mu_2=0$ ⇒ $\lambda=-2$, $\mu_1=2(0-2)+\lambda=-6\lt 0$. Reject (dual feasibility).
  5. $\mathcal A=\{2\}$: $v=0,u=1$, $\mu_1=0$ ⇒ $\lambda=2$, $\mu_2=\lambda=2\ge 0$. KKT point $(1,0)$, $\lambda^\star=2$, $\mu^\star=(0,2)$.
  6. $\mathcal A=\{1,2\}$: $u=v=0$ violates $u+v=1$. Impossible.
  7. LICQ at $(1,0)$: $\nabla h=(1,1)$, $\nabla g_2=(0,-1)$ independent ✓.
  8. $\mathcal A_+=\{2\}$, $\mathcal A_0=\varnothing$. $C=\{d: d_1+d_2=0,\ -d_2=0\}=\{0\}$.
  9. $L_\star=2I$ (constraints linear). $C=\{0\}$ ⇒ SOSC passes ⇒ $(1,0)$ is a strict local minimizer (global too: convex problem; it is the only KKT point).

Parametric version. When $f$ contains a parameter $a$, the active set changes with $a$. Method: do the enumeration with $a$ symbolic; each case gives a condition on $a$ ("$\mu\ge 0$ iff $a\ge 1$", "inactive iff $a\lt 1$"). Then treat each transition value separately: at the switch value the constraint is usually active with $\mu=0$ (it is in $\mathcal A_0$), so its row in $C$ is "$\le$", not "$=$". Full example: Midterm 2 IV and new question F1 below.

2.4 Active-set enumeration tricks

In plain words: with $p$ inequalities there are $2^p$ cases. Kill most of them fast:

  • Geometrically impossible sets: if forcing some $g_j=0$ together makes another constraint fail, that exact active set never occurs (Midterm 2 III.1: $g_2=g_3=0$ forces $x=0$ where $g_1=4\gt 0$).
  • Too many actives: more active constraints than unknowns usually has no solution, and if it does, check LICQ (more than $n$ gradients cannot be independent).
  • Sign contradictions: e.g. Midterm 2 IV: if $x_2\gt 0$ then $\mu_2=0$ and $(1+2\mu_1)x_2+1=0$ is impossible, so $x_2=0$ at every KKT point.
  • Homogeneity trick: dot stationarity with $x$ when $g$'s are linear and homogeneous: $2\|x\|^2+\mu_2g_2+\mu_3g_3=2\|x\|^2=0$ (Midterm 2 III.2).
  • Report infeasible roots and negative multipliers explicitly; the examiner gives marks for the rejections.

2.5 LICQ (regular point)

In plain words: the constraint gradients that matter at the point (all equalities + active inequalities) must be linearly independent. Then the linear picture of the constraints is faithful, KKT multipliers must exist at every local min, and they are unique.

Precisely (Def. 6.1.4.1): $x^\star$ is regular if $\{\nabla h_i(x^\star)\}_{i=1}^m\cup\{\nabla g_j(x^\star)\}_{j\in\mathcal A(x^\star)}$ is linearly independent, i.e. $\operatorname{rank}J(x^\star)=m+|\mathcal A(x^\star)|$. No active constraints ⇒ LICQ holds vacuously.

  • LICQ depends on the representation: $v=0$ is regular at the origin, $v^2=0$ (or $v^3=0$) is not, though the set is the same.
  • Under LICQ the multipliers are unique (full column rank). This does not mean the minimizer is unique.
  • LICQ is about gradient independence; strict complementarity ($\mathcal A_0=\varnothing$) is about multiplier signs. Different things: Midterm 2 IV at $a=1$ has LICQ but not strict complementarity.
Worked example Midterm 2 III at $(3,1)$: active $g_1,g_2$ with $\nabla g_1=(-1,-2x_2)=(-1,-2)$, $\nabla g_2=(-1,3)$. $\det\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\-2&3\end{pmatrix}=-3-2=-5\ne 0$ ⇒ LICQ holds.

2.6 Tangent cone, linearized cone, Farkas, geometric FONC

In plain words: the tangent cone $T_\Omega$ is the set of directions in which you can actually leave $x^\star$ while staying feasible (possibly along a curve). The linearized cone $\mathcal L_\Omega$ is what the constraint gradients claim are the feasible directions. The gradients can only over-promise, never under-promise: $T\subseteq\mathcal L$. At a regular point they agree, and then Farkas turns "no feasible descent direction" into "multipliers exist".

Precisely:

  • Def 6.1.2.1: $d\in T_\Omega(x^\star)$ iff there are $t_k\downarrow 0$, $d_k\to d$ with $x^\star+t_kd_k\in\Omega$.
  • Def 6.1.3.1: $\mathcal L_\Omega(x^\star)=\{d:\nabla h_i(x^\star)^\top d=0\ \forall i,\ \nabla g_j(x^\star)^\top d\le 0\ \forall j\in\mathcal A(x^\star)\}$ (closed convex cone).
  • Theorem 6.1.1: $T_\Omega\subseteq\mathcal L_\Omega$ always ($C^1$ constraints); $x^\star$ regular ⇒ $T_\Omega=\mathcal L_\Omega$. LICQ is sufficient, not necessary (e.g. $v=0$ and $2v=0$ together: dependent gradients, yet both cones are the horizontal axis). All constraints affine ⇒ $T=\mathcal L$.
  • $T_\Omega$ need not be convex or a subspace ($uv=0$: the union of the two axes).
  • Theorem 6.2.1 (fundamental FONC, no constraint qualification): $x^\star$ local min ⇒ $d^\top\nabla f(x^\star)\ge 0$ for every $d\in T_\Omega(x^\star)$.
  • Lemma 6.3.1 (Farkas): $d^\top c\ge 0$ for every $d$ with $A^\top d=0$, $B^\top d\le 0$ ⇔ $\exists\lambda$ (free), $\mu\ge 0$: $c+A\lambda+B\mu=0$. No rank assumption.
  • Chain to KKT: local min ⇒ FONC on $T$ ⇒ (regular: $T=\mathcal L$) FONC on $\mathcal L$ ⇒ (Farkas with $c=\nabla f$, $A=J_h$, $B=J_{g,\mathcal A}$) stationarity with $\mu_{\mathcal A}\ge 0$.
  • Reverse direction holds without LICQ: a KKT point always satisfies the tangent-cone FOC, because $T\subseteq\mathcal L$ and KKT gives $\nabla f^\top d\ge 0$ on all of $\mathcal L$.
Worked example (Quiz 3 Q12, linear constraints so T = L) $f=u+2v$ on $u+v+w=0$, $u\ge 0$, $v\le 1$. At the origin.
  1. Active: $-u\le 0$ (active), $v-1\le 0$ (inactive, $-1$).
  2. Linear constraints ⇒ $T=\mathcal L=\{(a,b,c): a+b+c=0,\ -a\le 0\}$.
  3. $\nabla f=(1,2,0)$. Try $d=(0,-1,1)$: in $T$ ($0+(-1)+1=0$, $a=0\ge 0$) and $\nabla f^\top d=-2\lt 0$ ⇒ FONC fails ⇒ origin is not a local min.
  4. $(-1,0,1)$ also gives $-1$ but is not tangent ($a=-1\lt 0$).
Trap He said he will not ask you to compute tangent cones for curved constraints, only linear ones (where $T=\mathcal L$). But MCQs test the facts: $T\subseteq\mathcal L$, which examples break equality, which sign Farkas uses.

2.7 Critical cone and the $\mathcal A_+/\mathcal A_0$ split

In plain words: at a KKT point, every linearized-feasible direction has first-order change $\nabla f^\top d\ge 0$. Directions with $\gt 0$ are settled (you go uphill). Only directions with first-order change exactly $0$ (the "ties") need a second-order look. Those are the critical directions. A constraint with $\mu_j\gt 0$ is "pushing", so moving off it strictly costs first-order increase: critical directions must stay on it ($=0$). A constraint with $\mu_j=0$ (active but not pushing) costs nothing to leave, so you may move inward ($\le 0$).

Precisely (Def 6.4.1.1): $C=\{d\in\mathcal L_\Omega(x^\star):\nabla g_j(x^\star)^\top d=0\ \forall j\in\mathcal A_+\}$, i.e. equalities for $h$ and $\mathcal A_+$, inequalities $\le 0$ for $\mathcal A_0$. At a KKT point $C=\{d\in\mathcal L_\Omega:\nabla f(x^\star)^\top d=0\}$, because $d^\top\nabla f=-\sum_{j\in\mathcal A_+}\mu_j^\star\nabla g_j^\top d\ge 0$ with equality iff each term vanishes. $C$ is a closed convex cone; it is a subspace when $\mathcal A_0=\varnothing$.

Worked example (notes Ex. 6.4.3.2) $f=u+v^2$ on $u\ge 0$, $v\ge 0$, at the origin. $g_1=-u$, $g_2=-v$.
  1. Stationarity: $(1,2v)+\mu_1(-1,0)+\mu_2(0,-1)=0$ at $(0,0)$ ⇒ $\mu_1=1$, $\mu_2=0$.
  2. $\mathcal A=\{1,2\}$, $\mathcal A_+=\{1\}$, $\mathcal A_0=\{2\}$.
  3. $\mathcal L_\Omega=\{a\ge 0,b\ge 0\}$; $C=\{-a=0,\ -b\le 0\}=\{(0,b):b\ge 0\}$ (a ray, not the line, not $\{0\}$).
  4. $L_\star=\operatorname{diag}(0,2)$; $d^\top L_\star d=2b^2\gt 0$ for $b\gt 0$ ⇒ SOSC ⇒ strict local min.
  5. Swap to $f=u-v^2$: same $\mu$ and $C$, but $d^\top L_\star d=-2b^2\lt 0$ ⇒ SONC fails ⇒ not a min (indeed $f(0,t)=-t^2$). If you had wrongly written $=0$ for the $\mathcal A_0$ row, $C$ would be $\{0\}$ and you would wrongly "pass" SOSC.

2.8 Second-order conditions (SONC, SOSC)

In plain words: on the tie directions, look at curvature, but the curvature of the Lagrangian, not of $f$: the constraint's own bending matters (a curved constraint can turn a hill into a valley along the feasible curve). And only on $C$, not on all of $\mathbb R^n$.

Precisely:

  • Theorem 6.4.1 (SONC): $f,h,g\in C^2$, $x^\star$ a local minimizer, $x^\star$ regular ⇒ KKT multipliers exist and $d^\top L_\star d\ge 0$ for every $d\in C$.
  • Theorem 6.4.2 (SOSC): $f,h,g\in C^2$, $(x^\star,\lambda^\star,\mu^\star)$ satisfies KKT, and $d^\top L_\star d\gt 0$ for every $0\ne d\in C$ ⇒ $x^\star$ is a strict local minimizer. No regularity needed.

Why $\mathcal A_0$ rows keep "$\le$": in the SONC proof you restrict to $\Omega_+$ (keep $\mathcal A_+$ constraints active); a zero-multiplier constraint contributes nothing to $\ell_\star-f$, so there is no reason to force it to stay active. Forcing "$=$" would throw away genuine feasible tie directions (the $u-v^2$ example above).

Worked example: constraint curvature (notes Ex. 6.4.3.1) $\min f=v-u^2$ s.t. $h=v-2u^2=0$, at the origin.
  1. $\nabla f=(0,1)$, $\nabla h=(0,1)\ne 0$ ⇒ regular. Stationarity $(0,1)+\lambda(0,1)=0$ ⇒ $\lambda^\star=-1$.
  2. $C=\{d:\nabla h^\top d=0\}=\{(a,0)\}$.
  3. $\nabla^2 f=\operatorname{diag}(-2,0)$ (negative on $C$!), $\nabla^2h=\operatorname{diag}(-4,0)$. $L_\star=\nabla^2f+\lambda^\star\nabla^2h=\operatorname{diag}(-2+4,0)=\operatorname{diag}(2,0)$.
  4. $d^\top L_\star d=2a^2\gt 0$ for $a\ne 0$ ⇒ SOSC ⇒ strict local min. Check: $f(u,2u^2)=u^2$. Using $\nabla^2 f$ alone would give the wrong answer.
Worked example: full Hessian indefinite, yet SOSC (Midterm 2 III corners) At $(3,1)$: $\mu=(4,2,0)$, both actives in $\mathcal A_+$, two independent rows ⇒ $C=\{0\}$. $L_\star=\operatorname{diag}(2,2-2\cdot 4)=\operatorname{diag}(2,-6)$ is indefinite, but there is no nonzero critical direction ⇒ SOSC passes ⇒ strict local min.

2.9 Eigenvector problems (practise every variant)

In plain words: stationarity always looks like $x=\mu Qx$ (or $Qx=\text{const}\cdot x$), so every boundary KKT point is an eigenvector scaled onto the constraint surface, and the multiplier is $1/\lambda_i$ (or $\lambda_i$). The critical cone at $\pm v_i$ is everything orthogonal to $v_i$, i.e. the span of the other eigenvectors. Then $d^\top L_\star d$ is a sum $\sum_{j\ne i}(\text{something with }\lambda_j-\lambda_i)\alpha_j^2$, and its sign picks out the smallest or largest eigenvalue.

Setup: $Q$ symmetric PD, distinct eigenvalues $0\lt\lambda_1\lt\dots\lt\lambda_n$, orthonormal $v_i$. Write $d=\sum_{j\ne i}\alpha_jv_j$.

Worked template: $\min x^\top x$ s.t. $x^\top Qx\ge 1$
  1. $g=1-x^\top Qx\le 0$. $\ell=x^\top x+\mu(1-x^\top Qx)$.
  2. KKT: $2x-2\mu Qx=0$; $x^\top Qx\ge 1$; $\mu\ge 0$; $\mu(1-x^\top Qx)=0$.
  3. Inactive ⇒ $\mu=0$ ⇒ $x=0$, infeasible. So active: $Qx=\tfrac1\mu x$ ⇒ $x=tv_i$, $\mu=1/\lambda_i\gt 0$, $\lambda_it^2=1$: $x_i^\pm=\pm v_i/\sqrt{\lambda_i}$, value $1/\lambda_i$.
  4. LICQ: $\nabla g=-2Qx=-2\lambda_ix\ne 0$ ✓. $\mu\gt 0$ ⇒ $C=\{d:v_i^\top d=0\}=\operatorname{span}\{v_j:j\ne i\}$.
  5. $L_\star=2(I-Q/\lambda_i)$, $d^\top L_\star d=2\sum_{j\ne i}(1-\lambda_j/\lambda_i)\alpha_j^2$.
  6. $i=n$: all $\lambda_j\lt\lambda_n$ ⇒ every coefficient $\gt 0$ ⇒ SOSC ⇒ strict local min. $i\lt n$: $d=v_n$ gives $2(1-\lambda_n/\lambda_i)\lt 0$ ⇒ SONC fails.
  7. Feasible set closed, $f$ coercive ⇒ global min exists ⇒ $\min=1/\lambda_n$ at $\pm v_n/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$.

2.11 The FONC proof (the only proof in the Ch 6 syllabus)

Statement (Theorem 6.2.1): $x^\star\in\Omega$ local minimizer, $f$ differentiable at $x^\star$ ⇒ $d^\top\nabla f(x^\star)\ge 0$ for all $d\in T_\Omega(x^\star)$. Proof chain:

  1. Take any $d\in T_\Omega(x^\star)$: there are $t_k\downarrow 0$, $d_k\to d$ with $x_k=x^\star+t_kd_k\in\Omega$; so $x_k\to x^\star$.
  2. Local min ⇒ for large $k$: $f(x_k)-f(x^\star)\ge 0$.
  3. Differentiability: $f(x_k)-f(x^\star)=t_kd_k^\top\nabla f(x^\star)+o(t_k)$; the remainder is $o(t_k)$ because $d_k$ is bounded ($R(t_kd_k)/t_k=\tfrac{R(t_kd_k)}{\|t_kd_k\|}\|d_k\|\to 0$).
  4. Divide by $t_k\gt 0$: $0\le d_k^\top\nabla f(x^\star)+o(1)$.
  5. Let $k\to\infty$: $d^\top\nabla f(x^\star)\ge 0$. No regularity used anywhere.

  4. $\lambda=4$: $x=\pm v_2/2=\pm(\tfrac{\sqrt2}4,\tfrac{\sqrt2}4)$, $\mu=\tfrac14$, $f=\tfrac14$. $\lambda=2$: $x=\pm v_1/\sqrt2=\pm(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$, $\mu=\tfrac12$, $f=\tfrac12$. All $\mu\gt 0$ ✓, all on $x^\top Qx=1$ ✓.
  5. LICQ. $\nabla g=-2Qx=-2\lambda_ix\ne 0$ ✓ at all four.
  6. Critical cones. $\mu\gt 0$ ⇒ $C=\{d:(Qx)^\top d=0\}=\{d:x^\top d=0\}$: at $\pm v_2/2$, $C=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1)\}$; at $\pm v_1/\sqrt2$, $C=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}$.
  7. $L_\star=2(I-\mu Q)$. At $\mu=\tfrac14$: $L_\star=\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac12&-\tfrac12\\-\tfrac12&\tfrac12\end{pmatrix}$, $d=(1,-1)$: $d^\top L_\star d=2\gt 0$ ⇒ SOSC ⇒ $\pm(\tfrac{\sqrt2}4,\tfrac{\sqrt2}4)$ strict local minimizers. At $\mu=\tfrac12$: $L_\star=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, $d=(1,1)$: $-4\lt 0$, regular ⇒ SONC fails ⇒ $\pm(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$ are not local minimizers.
  8. Global. Feasible set closed and nonempty, $f$ coercive ⇒ a global min exists; it is a KKT point (LICQ everywhere) ⇒ $\min=\tfrac14=1/\lambda_{\max}$ at $\pm(\tfrac{\sqrt2}4,\tfrac{\sqrt2}4)$.
  9. General. KKT points $\pm v_i/\sqrt{\lambda_i}$, $\mu=1/\lambda_i$; on $C=v_i^\perp$: $d^\top L_\star d=2\sum_{j\ne i}(1-\lambda_j/\lambda_i)\alpha_j^2$ ⇒ only $i=n$ passes (SOSC); answer $1/\lambda_n$ at $\pm v_n/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$.

5. Self-test (no looking)

S1

Write the Lagrangian and all five KKT conditions for $\min f$ s.t. $h=0$, $g\le 0$. Which multipliers have a sign restriction?

Show answer

$\ell=f+\lambda^\top h+\mu^\top g$. $\nabla f+J_h\lambda+J_g\mu=0$; $h=0$; $g\le 0$; $\mu\ge 0$; $\mu_jg_j=0\ \forall j$. Only $\mu\ge 0$; $\lambda$ free.

S2

Define LICQ. Under LICQ, what is unique and what is not?

Show answer

Gradients of all equalities and active inequalities linearly independent. The KKT multipliers are unique; the minimizer need not be.

S3

State the relation between $T_\Omega$ and $\mathcal L_\Omega$, and two cases where they are equal.

Show answer

$T\subseteq\mathcal L$ always ($C^1$ constraints). Equal at regular points (LICQ, sufficient not necessary) and whenever all constraints are affine.

S4

Write the critical cone with the $\mathcal A_+/\mathcal A_0$ split. Why do $\mathcal A_0$ rows keep "$\le$"?

Show answer

$C=\{d:\nabla h_i^\top d=0,\ \nabla g_j^\top d=0\ (j\in\mathcal A_+),\ \nabla g_j^\top d\le 0\ (j\in\mathcal A_0)\}$. A zero-multiplier constraint costs no first-order increase when you move inward, so those inward directions are still "ties" and must be tested; forcing "$=$" would lose them (e.g. $u-v^2$ on the quadrant).

S5

State SONC and SOSC with all hypotheses. Which one needs LICQ? Which gives a strict minimum?

Show answer

SONC: $C^2$, local min, regular ⇒ $d^\top L_\star d\ge 0$ on $C$ (needs LICQ). SOSC: $C^2$, KKT holds, $d^\top L_\star d\gt 0$ for all $0\ne d\in C$ ⇒ strict local min (no LICQ).

S6

$C=\{0\}$ at a KKT point. Does SOSC pass or fail? What if $L_\star$ is indefinite?

Show answer

Passes (vacuously) ⇒ strict local min. Indefinite $L_\star$ on $\mathbb R^n$ is irrelevant; only $C$ matters.

S7

State Farkas' lemma in the course form (signs!).

Show answer

$d^\top c\ge 0$ for all $d$ with $A^\top d=0$, $B^\top d\le 0$ ⇔ $\exists\lambda$ free, $\mu\ge 0$ with $c+A\lambda+B\mu=0$.

S8

Give one example each: (i) a minimum where KKT fails; (ii) an active constraint with $\mu=0$; (iii) KKT + SONC hold, SOSC fails, not a min.

Show answer

(i) $\min x$ s.t. $-x^3\le 0$ at $0$. (ii) $\min x^2$ s.t. $-x\le 0$ at $0$. (iii) $\min v-u^4$ s.t. $-v\le 0$ at the origin ($\mu=1$, $C=\{(a,0)\}$, $L_\star=0$, $f(t,0)=-t^4$).
